How to Read a Greige Fabric Specification Sheet Before Placing an Order

Buying greige fabric is not simply about choosing a fabric that looks or feels right.

When an order moves from sampling to bulk production, the details written on the specification sheet become extremely important.

A good greige fabric specification gives the manufacturer and buyer a common technical reference.

It reduces ambiguity, makes quotations easier to compare and helps ensure that the fabric being produced matches the intended construction.

But what exactly should you look for on a greige fabric specification sheet?

Let's break it down.

1. Fabric Construction

Construction is one of the first things a buyer should understand.

For woven fabric, this can include the warp and weft yarn counts, EPI (Ends Per Inch), PPI (Picks Per Inch), weave and width.

Together, these details provide a much clearer picture of how the fabric is constructed than simply describing it as “lightweight” or “medium weight.” For example, a specification might mention: 40s × 40s / 110 × 90 This indicates the yarn count used in the two directions and the fabric's ends and picks per inch.

The exact combination matters because changing one element can change the resulting fabric.

2. GSM

GSM means grams per square metre and indicates the weight of the fabric.

However, GSM should not be treated as a direct measure of fabric quality.

A higher GSM does not automatically mean a better fabric.

It simply means more fabric mass exists per square metre.

Two fabrics can have similar GSM values but different yarn counts, construction and hand feel.

When specifying GSM, it is also useful to clarify whether the number refers to greige fabric or finished fabric, because processing can change the final weight.

3. Yarn Count

Yarn count describes the fineness or thickness of the yarn.

In commonly used cotton count systems, a higher count generally represents a finer yarn.

But yarn count should always be considered alongside the rest of the construction rather than viewed in isolation.

A fabric's final character comes from the interaction between yarn, density, weave and processing.

4. EPI and PPI

EPI refers to the number of warp yarns per inch, while PPI refers to the number of weft yarns per inch.

These numbers help describe how densely the yarns are arranged in the fabric and can influence its cover, appearance, weight and handle.

5. Width

Width is another specification that should never be treated as an afterthought.

The usable width required depends on the final application.

A difference between nominal and usable width can affect cutting efficiency, consumption and ultimately production cost.

Therefore, the width requirement and its acceptable tolerance should be clearly communicated before bulk production.

6. Composition and Yarn Details

The specification should also clearly state the fibre composition and relevant yarn details.

For blended fabrics especially, the blend ratio can directly affect the fabric's characteristics and subsequent processing.

7. Quantity and Tolerance

Finally, a practical specification should make clear: ● Required quantity ● GSM ● Width ● Construction ● Yarn count ● Composition ● Applicable tolerances ● Approved sample/reference This is particularly important for repeat orders.

A written specification and retained approved sample give both sides something concrete to work against rather than relying on “same as the previous order.”

Why a Detailed Specification Matters

A specification sheet is more than a list of numbers.

It is the technical language between a fabric buyer and a manufacturer.

The clearer the requirement, the easier it becomes to evaluate quotations, produce the right construction and maintain consistency across production.
